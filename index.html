Biomechanics & Logical Evolution

The Art of No-Fins

A methodology built on movement logic. We confront the classic approach with the new standard (v1.0), eliminating bottlenecks and energy waste through precise body alignment and ruthless timing.

Versioning Methodology: Classic vs v1.0

Swimming is mechanics. By analyzing the world's best (Classic Execution), logical energy losses become evident (e.g., initiating a kick before the arms are fully streamlined). Version v1.0 (The Art of No-Fins) is a collection of solutions patching these exact logical gaps. It is my definitive standard.

Experimental ideas (e.g., v1.1, v1.2) are open tests. They are logged and shared with the community as hypotheses to be tested, not as mandatory dogma.

The Core Evolution: 4 Pillars

Pillar 1: Legs & Hips Propulsion

Presentation
CLASSIC EXECUTION

The Wide Frog Kick

Knees flare out widely to the sides during the recovery phase. This relies on external hip rotation, preparing the legs for a powerful, but highly drag-inducing, outward thrust.

v1.0 STANDARD

Narrow Knee Track (Internal Rotation)

Knees remain hidden within the strict shadow of the chest. Feet flare outward from the hips via internal rotation, loading tension for a narrow, straight-backward Whip Kick.

Logical Gain

Drastic reduction in Frontal Area drag. The thighs no longer act as a massive parachute slowing the diver down while drawing the legs up for the kick.

Pillar 2: Stroke Timing & Sequencing

Presentation
CLASSIC EXECUTION

Premature Drive Initiation

Even world-class athletes frequently initiate the powerful downward leg kick while their arms are still traveling forward—often bent in front of the face or flared to the sides.

v1.0 STANDARD

Absolute Arrow Lock Before Drive

A strict, uncompromising rule: The propulsive phase of the kick ("Drive") must never begin until the arms are fully locked in a perfect, straight arrow overhead.

Logical Gain

100% of the leg kick energy is translated into forward glide through an optimal streamline. Mismatching these phases is logically akin to driving a car with the handbrake engaged.

Pillar 3: Axial Glide & Posture

Presentation
CLASSIC EXECUTION

The "Resting Phase" & Compensations

Glide is theoretically understood, but executed as a relaxed "resting phase." This causes core relaxation resulting in lumbar lordosis (banana back), the head drifting up out of alignment, and panic-compensations like throwing in an illegal "dolphin kick" to maintain sinking speed.

v1.0 STANDARD

Active Hollow Body Lock

Glide is an active state requiring discipline. The pelvis is tucked (posterior tilt) to flatten the lower back. The head is strictly stowed between shoulders. Feet are dead and glued together. Absolute ban on dolphin kick compensations.

Logical Gain

Elimination of micro-drag. The body maintained as a rigid, flat object (Hollow Body) minimizes the vertical frontal profile. Water flows cleanly over the back instead of slamming into a protruding chest or dropped legs.

Pillar 4: Arms Pull & Catch

Presentation
CLASSIC EXECUTION

The Deep Sweep Pull

A wide sweeping motion ("keyhole" pull) or dragging the arms deeply underneath the body. This relies on the natural, dropping movement of the shoulder without locking the elbow structure early.

v1.0 STANDARD

Early Vertical Forearm (High Elbow)

Immediate positioning of the forearms vertical to the direction of travel (90 degrees). The elbow remains high, transforming the entire hand and forearm into a single, massive propulsive surface anchored in the water.

Logical Gain

Maximizes the force vector directed strictly backward. Instead of pushing water downwards (which lifts the body) or outwards (which stalls momentum), we pull the body forward along a stable, anchored line.

🧪 Open Lab (Experimental Testing)

Not Standard

This is where hypotheses meet the water. Experimental versions (e.g., v1.1, v1.2) are attempts to enhance the established v1.0 standard. They often fail or prove inefficient in deep freediving context, but they are archived here as "open-source" ideas for the community to test, debate, or adapt to different anatomies.

Legs v1.1 Test Hard to Execute

Sole Clapping Finish

Hypothesis: forcefully snapping the soles of the feet together at the very end of the Whip Kick drive will generate an additional burst of backward water flow.

Lab Conclusion: In shallow pool conditions (DNF), a micro-acceleration is felt. However, the effort disrupts the return to a relaxed arrow position. In deep freediving (CNF), where freefall balance is paramount, this forced clap destroys the relaxed profile. Left open for DNF sprinters.
Arms v1.1 Test In Progress

Micro-Gaps (Spread Fingers)

Hypothesis: Inspired by CFD (Computational Fluid Dynamics) analysis of elite freestyle swimmers. Leaving micro-gaps (approx. 2-3 mm) between fingers increases the effective surface area of the hand due to boundary layer fluid dynamics.

Lab Conclusion: Difficult to execute instinctively without stiffening the wrists. Underwater catch tests are ongoing. Requires isolated pool work to measure true stroke count reduction vs effort.
